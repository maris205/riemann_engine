## Title

A Four-Anchor Phenomenological Fit of a 1/ln^2(t/t_P) Hubble Relaxation

## Abstract

We study a selected phenomenological ansatz in which a Hubble rate varies as 1/ln^2(t/t_P). The functional form is a choice of fitting function. It is not derived from a microscopic theory, and it is not claimed to be the unique slow function compatible with laboratory and cosmological bounds. The core empirical result is a two-parameter curve through four assigned (t, H) anchors. The fit quoted in the submitted manuscript is H_infinity = 104.5 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, beta = -6.24 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, R^2 = 0.91, and chi^2/dof = 0.28 on two degrees of freedom, with the regressor normalized as 10^5/ln^2(t/t_P). That normalization reconciles the quoted intercept with the quoted finite-redshift values near 71-72 km s^-1 Mpc^-1: H_infinity is the t to infinity intercept of the curve, and the high-redshift numbers are values of the same curve at finite t. The statistical weight of four points is limited. Parameter covariance and leave-one-anchor-out tests are not yet reported, and the prose list of anchors in the submitted manuscript does not match the four inputs of the figure notebook. Quasar comparisons of Delta alpha/alpha on the Murphy et al. (2003) and King et al. (2012) samples are exploratory. The submitted text quotes incompatible Murphy-sample coefficients, +6.42 +/- 1.14 and +5.56 +/- 0.99, and the Keck-only and VLT-only subsets prefer opposite signs. No detection is claimed. Dense late-time samples return chi^2/dof values from order unity up to about 10^3. Those values are poor or worse fits of those samples. The Riemann-zeta motivation and the lattice CMB forward model, inverse reconstruction, and acoustic freeze-out analysis have been removed.
