# Title and abstract (plain text, for Editorial Manager)

## Title

Phenomenological Fits of a 1/ln^2(t/t_*) Form to the Hubble Constant and the Fine-Structure Constant

The submitted title was "Differentiable Discrete Symplectic Cosmology: Cross-Scale Empirical Tests of a 1/ln^2(t) Cooling Law". The approved title names the two fitted quantities, the Hubble constant and the fine-structure constant, and describes a phenomenological form rather than a named cooling law.

## Abstract

We test a phenomenological cooling law H(t) = H_infinity + beta * xi(t), with xi(t) = 10^5 / ln^2(t/t_*), against four published inferences of the Hubble constant placed at assigned epochs, and we compare that functional form with flat LambdaCDM and the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) parameterization on one cosmic-chronometer sample. The same 1/ln^2 shape is applied, with an independent amplitude, to the fractional variation of the fine-structure constant. The amplitude Gamma is defined once, by delta_alpha(t) = Gamma [L(t)^{-2} - L(t_0)^{-2}], and the present drift follows by differentiation, D_0 = -2 Gamma / (t_0 L_0^3). On the four anchors the relaxation fit gives H_infinity = 104.47 +/- 7.05 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, beta = -6.243 +/- 1.226, an unweighted R^2 = 0.907 and chi^2 = 0.559 for two degrees of freedom. Leave-one-anchor-out shows that the slope is not stable when the Planck anchor is removed. On 32 cosmic chronometers the same functional form, refitted, has chi^2 = 20.14, against chi^2 = 14.57 for flat LambdaCDM and chi^2 = 14.33 for flat CPL; CPL is not preferred once its two extra parameters are penalized. A quasar comparison is reported only as an exploratory check. Under one shared Gamma, the Murphy et al. (2003) fit-error amplitude is in tension with the Filzinger et al. (2023) atomic-clock constraint. This paper does not claim a detection of evolving H_0 or of a cosmological drift of alpha.
