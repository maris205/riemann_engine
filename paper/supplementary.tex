\section{Exploratory quasar figures}
\label{sec:sm_alpha}

\paragraph{Full King et al.~(2012) catalog}
The submitted comparison on the full King et al.~(2012) catalog quotes reduced $\chi^2 = 0.881$ for the ansatz and $0.878$ for a constant, with $\Delta\chi^2\approx 0$ and $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=+5.7$ in favor of the constant (\cref{fig:sm_king2012}).
This is the full-sample end of the side-by-side comparison.
It is not a detection.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig20_quasar_293_fit.pdf}
  \caption{Exploratory three-model comparison on the full King et al.~(2012) sample, as plotted for the submitted manuscript.
  The submitted text quotes $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=+5.7$ in favor of a constant.
  The joint numerical bound drawn on the figure was not recomputed.}
  \label{fig:sm_king2012}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Keck-only, VLT-only, and random subsets}
The submitted subset scan quotes a positive Keck-only coefficient and a negative VLT-only coefficient (\cref{fig:sm_pillar_K}).
The scan is exploratory: the repository does not describe a pre-registered subset rule.
The Murphy-sample coefficient that this scan was compared against is one of the two unreconciled values in the main text.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig27_pillar_K_subset.pdf}
  \caption{Exploratory King et al.~(2012) subset scan as plotted for the submitted manuscript.
  Keck-only and VLT-only subsets are shown side by side with random draws of 128 absorbers.
  The figure is a subset comparison, and it is not a detection.}
  \label{fig:sm_pillar_K}
\end{figure}

\section{Late-time sample figures}
\label{sec:sm_late}

The figures in this section are the plots behind the main-text summary of previously quoted late-time fits.
Captions state the submitted $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$.
They do not treat a poor fit as support for the ansatz.

\paragraph{Compressed Fisher check}
The submitted Fisher matrix, built from compressed Planck observables and DESI BAO ratios, quotes $\sigma(\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}})\sim 1.2\times 10^{4}$ (\cref{fig:sm_camb_fisher}).
That width means the compressed data did not measure the amplitude $\gamma$.
The check uses a Boltzmann code on background distances.
It is not a lattice simulation, and it is not evidence that the ansatz matches the CMB spectrum.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig21_camb_fisher.pdf}
  \caption{Submitted compressed Fisher illustration for $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
  The quoted combined width is $\sigma(\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}})\sim 1.2\times 10^{4}$.
  The figure does not show a lattice CMB spectrum.}
  \label{fig:sm_camb_fisher}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Background checks}
The submitted scan reports that five background inequalities pass for $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\in[0,10]$ and $a\in[10^{-10},1]$ (\cref{fig:sm_late_stability}).
The scan does not test perturbative stability or gradient instabilities.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig22_late_stability.pdf}
  \caption{Submitted background-check scan.
  A pass of these five inequalities is not a stability proof for the ansatz.}
  \label{fig:sm_late_stability}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Cosmic chronometers}
The submitted comparison quotes $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=0.49$ for flat $\Lambda$CDM and $4.01$ for the ansatz (\cref{fig:sm_cc}).
The ansatz value is a worse goodness of fit on this sample.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig23_cc_hz_pillar_F.pdf}
  \caption{Cosmic-chronometer $H(z)$ figure from the submitted manuscript.
  The quoted $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ is $0.49$ for flat $\Lambda$CDM and $4.01$ for the ansatz.}
  \label{fig:sm_cc}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Pantheon+}
The submitted Pantheon+ comparison quotes $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=0.88$ for flat $\Lambda$CDM and $1.27$ for the ansatz, with $\Delta\chi^2=+617$ (\cref{fig:sm_pantheon}).
The Gaussian-equivalent conversion of that $\Delta\chi^2$ is not used.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig24_pantheon_pillar_G.pdf}
  \caption{Pantheon+ figure from the submitted manuscript.
  The quoted $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ is $0.88$ for flat $\Lambda$CDM and $1.27$ for the ansatz.}
  \label{fig:sm_pantheon}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{DESI DR2}
The submitted DESI DR2 comparison quotes $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=1.07$ for flat $\Lambda$CDM and a $\chi^2$ of order $10^{4}$ for the ansatz (\cref{fig:sm_desi})~\citep{DESI2025DR2}.
The corresponding $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ quoted in the main text is $\sim 10^{3}$.
This is a poor fit of the DESI DR2 sample.
The citation is the DR2 paper, not the 2024 Year-1 analysis.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig25_desi_pillar_H.pdf}
  \caption{DESI DR2 BAO figure from the submitted manuscript~\citep{DESI2025DR2}.
  The quoted ansatz $\chi^2$ is far above the flat $\Lambda$CDM value.
  That discrepancy is a poor fit.}
  \label{fig:sm_desi}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Joint late-time set}
The submitted joint comparison quotes $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=0.96$ for flat $\Lambda$CDM and $23.7$ for the ansatz (\cref{fig:sm_pillar_J}).
A submitted $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=-2.6$ between CPL and flat $\Lambda$CDM is a statement about those two parametrizations in the submitted analysis.
It is not a completed comparison against the main-text Hubble equation.
