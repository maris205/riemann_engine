\begin{abstract}
We study a selected phenomenological ansatz in which a Hubble rate varies as $1/\ln^2(t/\tP)$.
The functional form is a choice of fitting function.
It is not derived from a microscopic theory, and it is not claimed to be the unique slow function compatible with laboratory and cosmological bounds.
The core empirical result is a two-parameter curve through four assigned $(t,H)$ anchors.
The fit quoted in the submitted manuscript is $H_\infty = 104.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $\beta = -6.24\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $R^2 = 0.91$, and $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} = 0.28$ on two degrees of freedom, with the regressor normalized as $10^5/\ln^2(t/\tP)$.
That normalization reconciles the quoted intercept with the quoted finite-redshift values near $71$--$72\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$: $H_\infty$ is the $t\to\infty$ intercept of the curve, and the high-redshift numbers are values of the same curve at finite $t$.
The statistical weight of four points is limited.
Parameter covariance and leave-one-anchor-out tests are not yet reported, and the prose list of anchors in the submitted manuscript does not match the four inputs of the figure notebook.
Quasar comparisons of $\Delta\alpha/\alpha$ on the Murphy et al.\ (2003) and King et al.\ (2012) samples are exploratory.
The submitted text quotes incompatible Murphy-sample coefficients, $+6.42\pm 1.14$ and $+5.56\pm 0.99$, and the Keck-only and VLT-only subsets prefer opposite signs.
No detection is claimed.
Dense late-time samples return $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ values from order unity up to $\sim 10^3$.
Those values are poor or worse fits of those samples.
The Riemann-zeta motivation and the lattice CMB forward model, inverse reconstruction, and acoustic freeze-out analysis have been removed.
\end{abstract}
