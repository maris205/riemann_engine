\subsection{Four-anchor Hubble fit}
\label{sec:fit-method}

The notebook \path{notebooks/fig19_jwst_h0_anchors.ipynb} builds four pairs $(t_i,H_i)$ with quoted uncertainties $\sigma_i$, forms $\xi_i = 1/\ln^2(t_i/\tP)$, and fits \cref{eq:hubble} by weighted least squares (\path{scipy.optimize.curve_fit} with \path{absolute_sigma=True}).
The reported coefficient of determination is the unweighted quantity
\begin{equation}
  R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_i (H_i-\hat H_i)^2}{\sum_i (H_i-\bar H)^2}\,,
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2 = \sum_i \left(\frac{H_i-\hat H_i}{\sigma_i}\right)^2\,,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{dof} = N-2 = 2\,,
\end{equation}
with $N=4$ anchors and two free parameters.
The notebook stores the parameter covariance and does not print it.
No leave-one-anchor-out loop is present.
\revtodo{Report the $2\times 2$ covariance and the four leave-one-anchor-out refits, using the anchor list confirmed in \cref{sec:pillarC}. Do not insert those numbers until the refit has been run and checked.}

The four inputs coded in that notebook are listed in \cref{tab:anchors}.
They are not a new fit.
The submitted prose instead named SH0ES~\citep{RiessSH0ES2022}, a JWST Cepheid analysis~\citep{RiessJWST2024}, Planck~\citep{Planck2018VI}, and a DESI BAO inference~\citep{DESI2024}.
Those citations and the notebook inputs are not the same list.
Several of the numbers are published inferences of a present-day $H_0$, assigned in the notebook to widely different cosmic times.
The Planck value $67.4\pm 0.5$ is a $\Lambda$CDM inference of the present-day Hubble constant~\citep{Planck2018VI}.
It is not a measurement of the expansion rate at $t = 3.8\times 10^{5}\,\mathrm{yr}$.
The fit is a curve through four assigned pairs.
Reading the curve as the expansion history $H(t)$ is an additional interpretation, and it is not established by the assignment of those times.

\subsection{Quasar comparison}
\label{sec:alpha-method}

The exploratory comparison uses the Murphy, Webb, and Flambaum~(2003) Keck/HIRES table~\citep{Murphy2003} and the King et al.~(2012) Keck+VLT catalog~\citep{King2012}.
The submitted analysis quoted two error budgets for the Murphy sample: budget~(i), the per-absorber fit error alone, and budget~(ii), that fit error added in quadrature to Murphy et al.'s published random term.
The same manuscripts quoted Keck-only, VLT-only, and combined subsets.
No pre-registered or blind subset protocol is described in the repository.
The subset split is therefore exploratory.
Both of the readings left open by Reviewer~1 remain open: a Keck-specific instrumental systematic~\citep{Whitmore2015,Wilczynska2020}, or a fluctuation of the chosen subsample.
This revision does not adjudicate them.
\revtodo{Reconcile the Murphy-sample coefficients $+6.42\pm 1.14$ and $+5.56\pm 0.99$ by a single refit, and quote one $\Gamma$ per row of \cref{tab:alpha} under a stated error budget. Until that refit, neither value is the result.}

\subsection{Late-time samples and the forecast}
\label{sec:late-method}

Cosmic-chronometer, Pantheon$+$, and DESI DR2 comparisons were reported in the submitted manuscript and are summarized in \cref{sec:dense} as previously quoted $\chi^2$ values~\citep{Moresco2022,Scolnic2022,Brout2022,DESI2025DR2}.
They were not recomputed for this revision.
The DESI DR2 comparison uses the DR2 publication~\citep{DESI2025DR2}.
The 2024 DESI BAO paper~\citep{DESI2024} is a Year-1 analysis and is cited only as background.
A uniform likelihood for the ansatz, flat $\Lambda$CDM, and the Chevallier--Polarski--Linder (CPL) parametrization, with AIC and BIC on that common likelihood, is not provided.
\revtodo{Recompute $\chi^2$, AIC, and BIC for the ansatz, flat $\Lambda$CDM, and CPL on one stated dataset and covariance. Do not reuse the submitted $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -2.6$ as that comparison.}

The forecast notebook \path{notebooks/fig31_pillar_O_forecast.ipynb} converts redshift to age with a flat $\Lambda$CDM integral ($H_0 = 67.4$, $\Omega_m = 0.315$), evaluates \cref{eq:hubble} at the quoted $(H_\infty,\beta) = (104.5,-6.24)$, and draws mock $H(z)$ points with independent Gaussian errors equal to a constant fraction of the true $H$.
It uses \texttt{numpy}'s Generator with seed 42, 30 realizations, and Nelder--Mead refits of both curves.
The submitted ``significance'' is $\sqrt{\Delta\chi^2}$ of the median realization.
That construction has no survey window, no systematic floor, and no off-diagonal covariance.
The resulting figures are recorded in \cref{sec:pillarO} so that the calculation can be inspected, and they are not claimed as expected detection significances.
\revtodo{Replace the forecast with a calculation that states the redshift sampling, the covariance, and the systematic floor. Do not promote the existing $\sqrt{\Delta\chi^2}$ values to a predicted significance.}
