\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Local and early-Universe inferences of the Hubble constant disagree when they are interpreted as measurements of one constant~\citep{DiValentino2021,Schoneberg2022}.
Planck, analysed in flat $\Lambda$CDM, gives $67.4\pm0.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$~\citep{Planck2018VI}.
The SH0ES Cepheid ladder gives $73.04\pm1.04\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$~\citep{RiessSH0ES2022}.
A separate literature asks whether an effective $H_{0}$ estimated in redshift bins declines with redshift~\citep{Krishnan2020,Dainotti2021,Colgain2022}.
Those trends are not, by themselves, a model-independent detection of new physics.

This paper asks a narrower question.
Suppose one adopts the two-parameter form
\begin{equation}
  H(t)=H_{\infty}+\beta\,\xi(t),
  \qquad
  \xi(t)\equiv\frac{10^{5}}{\ln^{2}(t/t_{*})},
  \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
with a fixed reference scale $t_{*}$ and with $\beta$ multiplying $\xi$, not $1/\ln^{2}$.
Does this form describe four published $H_{0}$ inferences once each is assigned an epoch, and does the same shape describe the expansion rate $H(z)$ measured by cosmic chronometers?
The two questions are different.
The first uses an epoch assignment that is an assumption of the fit.
The second uses $H(z)$ measurements and does not use that assignment.

Equation~\eqref{eq:H} is a phenomenological ansatz.
The previous version of this manuscript motivated the exponent by the spectral statistics of the Riemann zeta zeros and accompanied the fit with a lattice model of the cosmic microwave background.
Both of those developments have been removed.
No uniqueness of $1/\ln^{2}$ is claimed.
A power of $\ln(t/t_{*})$ is one slowly varying function among others, and the anchor sample is too small to select it.

The same shape is used for a second, independent amplitude in the fine-structure constant.
Section~\ref{sec:clock} derives the present-day drift from that amplitude.
The derivation fixes the relation between a cosmological difference and a laboratory derivative.
It does not by itself make a quasar best fit compatible with atomic clocks.

The quasar material is exploratory.
Keck and VLT subsets of \citep{King2012} prefer amplitudes of opposite sign, the Murphy and King Keck rows are largely the same absorbers, and no pre-registered subset protocol is claimed.
The conclusions in \cref{sec:conclusion} are limited to what the calculations show.
