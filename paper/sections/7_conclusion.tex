\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A single convention is used throughout.
The Hubble fit is $H=H_{\infty}+\beta\,\xi$ with $\xi=10^{5}/\ln^{2}(t/t_{*})$, giving $H_{\infty}=104.47\pm7.05\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\beta=-6.243\pm1.226$ on four epoch-assigned $H_{0}$ inferences.
$H_{\infty}$ is the infinite-time limit of that line.
The line at the assigned present epoch is $72.73\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$.
Leave-one-anchor-out shows that the slope depends on retaining the Planck anchor.
On 32 cosmic chronometers, flat $\Lambda$CDM fits better than the same two-parameter form, and flat CPL does not improve on $\Lambda$CDM once AIC or BIC is applied.
The anchor parameters are a poor description of those chronometers.

For the fine-structure constant, $\Gamma$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha} replaces the two inconsistent values quoted previously.
The flat-universe age gives $\Gamma=-6.422\pm1.143$ on the Murphy fit errors.
The Einstein--de~Sitter age, which produced the old abstract value, is not used as a result.
Differentiation gives $D_{0}=\kappa\Gamma$ with $\kappa=-5.256\times10^{-17}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$.
The Murphy fit-error amplitude and the Filzinger et al.\ (2023) clock summary differ by $5.61$ standard deviations if they measure the same $\Gamma$.
Keck and VLT subsets prefer opposite signs.
That comparison is exploratory.

The Riemann-zeta motivation and the lattice CMB calculation have been removed.
The results above do not establish a physical cooling law, do not resolve the Hubble tension, and do not detect a cosmological change in $\alpha$.
A useful next measurement is one that determines $H(z)$ where Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} and $\Lambda$CDM separate by more than the chronometer lever arm, with a covariance and an epoch assignment that are specified before the fit.
