\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Reading of the four-anchor fit}
\label{sec:disc-fit}

The numerical content of Pillar~C is a two-parameter curve that passes close to four assigned $(t,H)$ pairs, with the quoted $R^2$ and $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ of \cref{sec:pillarC}.
The phenomenological reading, that the cosmic expansion rate has followed \cref{eq:hubble}, needs two further steps that this fit does not supply.
The first is an agreement on which four measurements are the anchors: the notebook inputs in \cref{tab:anchors} and the prose list SH0ES, JWST, Planck, and BAO are not the same list.
The second is a justification for placing present-day $H_0$ inferences at the assigned times.
In particular, the Planck $\Lambda$CDM inference of the present-day Hubble constant is not the expansion rate at recombination.
Until those steps are supplied, the fit remains a description of the assigned pairs.
The intercept $H_\infty = 104.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ is the $t\to\infty$ intercept of that description under the $10^{5}$ normalization.
It is larger than the anchor values because $\beta$ is negative.

\subsection{Relation to other $H_0(z)$ fits}
\label{sec:disc-literature}

Several groups have fitted smooth trends in low-redshift distance data, typically with a power of $(1+z)$, and have reported mild preferences~\citep{Dainotti2021,Krishnan2020,Kazantzidis2020,Colgain2022}.
The Hubble-tension census of Sch\"oneberg et al.~\citep{Schoneberg2022} reviews a wide range of physical proposals.
Equation~\eqref{eq:hubble} is a different fitting function from those power laws.
The dynamic range of $\xi(t)$ inside $z<2$ is small (\cref{sec:pillarM}), so a mild low-redshift trend neither confirms nor excludes the four-anchor curve.
The late-time $\chi^2$ values in \cref{tab:dense} already show that the same function is a poor description of DESI DR2 and a worse description than flat $\Lambda$CDM of the other listed samples.
A published CPL preference on BAO and supernova compilations is a statement about CPL.
The submitted $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=-2.6$ is not reused here as a comparison against \cref{eq:hubble}.

\subsection{Quasar subsets and the laboratory bound}
\label{sec:disc-alpha}

The exploratory table shows a Keck-only positive coefficient, a VLT-only negative coefficient, and a combined coefficient closer to zero, together with an unresolved clash between $+6.42\pm 1.14$ and $+5.56\pm 0.99$ on the Murphy sample.
Whitmore and Murphy~\citep{Whitmore2015} and Wilczynska et al.~\citep{Wilczynska2020} document instrumental contributions that can move a Keck $\Delta\alpha/\alpha$ inference.
That literature makes an instrumental reading available.
It does not, on the evidence assembled here, exclude a subset fluctuation.
The laboratory translation in \cref{sec:pillarN} limits how large $|\Gamma|$ can be if \cref{eq:alpha} is assumed.
It does not show that the quasar catalogs have measured a nonzero value inside that limit.
