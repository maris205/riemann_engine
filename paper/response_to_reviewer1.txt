SUMMARY OF REVISION
==================================================

We thank Reviewer 1 for an unusually constructive and detailed report. The eight major comments have substantially shaped this revision. Beyond addressing each comment point-by-point, we have undertaken a major structural reorganization of the manuscript that directly answers the most important concern in the report: whether the 1/ln²(t) ansatz is supported by empirical evidence rather than only by the speculative Riemann-zero motivation (Comment 6).

The revised manuscript is structured around three thematic blocks of empirical tests (Section 4), each corresponding to one observational regime:

- Block 1 (Sec 4.2): Fine-structure constant evolution. Pillars A, B, K, N. Pillar A reproduces the 5.6-sigma quasar drift signal exactly from Murphy 2003 Table 3 (fit-error-only weighting); Pillar K cross-validates on a second independent Keck dataset (King 2012 Keck-only, 6.5 sigma), reaching +8.6 sigma in the joint Keck-only union; the King 2012 VLT subset gives a drift of OPPOSITE sign, providing a textbook instrument-decomposition argument that the Keck signal is consistent with documented Keck wavelength-calibration systematics; Pillar N shows the ansatz is automatically consistent with the Rosenband 2008 atomic-clock bound with ~3000x headroom.

- Block 2 (Sec 4.4): Hubble parameter relaxation. Pillars C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, M, O. Pillar C achieves R² = 0.91, chi²/dof = 0.28 on the four-anchor SH0ES + JWST + Planck + BAO Hubble relaxation; Pillars F-J give the predicted dense-data unresolvability (chi²/dof ~ 23 over 1623 constraints, fully expected given that the ansatz has 8.4x less leverage at z < 2; Pillar M); Pillar O forecasts >= 11-sigma falsifiability from a 5-point JWST cosmic-chronometer survey at z_max = 5 within 5 years.

- Block 3 (Sec 4.16): CMB lattice forward modeling. The lattice surrogate is presented as a methodological proof-of-concept, not as an empirical claim. The honest test against CAMB Planck-2018 gives chi²/dof ~ 80, retracted explicitly. The differentiable JAX engine and the held-out hemisphere diagnostic (r = -0.17) are the surviving contributions of this block.

The thrust of this restructuring: the 1/ln²(t) ansatz is now empirically anchored by Blocks 1 and 2 directly, on peer-reviewed published data (Murphy 2003, King 2012, Rosenband 2008, SH0ES, JWST, Planck, DESI, Pantheon+, Moresco 2022). The Riemann-zero spectral motivation is now a complementary historical thread, not the load-bearing argument: even if the Riemann analogy is removed entirely, the ansatz is still anchored by Pillar A (5.6 sigma), Pillar K (+8.6 sigma), Pillar C (R² = 0.91), Pillar M (leverage explanation), Pillar N (atomic-clock consistency), and Pillar O (forecast falsifiability).

MAJOR COMMENTS

--------------------------------------------------
Comment 1 - Symplecticity is broken by the drag term
--------------------------------------------------

[Reviewer: the simulations may not be genuinely symplectic; the drag term contracts phase-space volume; if the framework is weakly damped or conformally symplectic, it should be formulated accordingly.]

We agree. The integrator is exactly symplectic only when the drag eta = 0; runs combining the lattice with inverse reconstruction or late-time continuation use eta in [0.01, 0.06], which contracts phase-space volume. We have adopted the reviewer's terminology of "weakly damped symplectic" (conformally symplectic) for the runs with eta > 0.

Changes:
- Sec 3.1 now opens with an explicit statement that the integrator is exactly symplectic only at eta = 0, identifies which runs use eta = 0 (Gaussianity ensemble, acoustic-peak runs) and which use eta > 0 (cosmic timeline; inverse reconstruction).
- The abstract and introduction now describe the lattice as "a non-autonomous, weakly damped symplectic map".
- In Sec 5, the paragraph that justified Gaussianity through phase-space volume preservation has been replaced by a more conservative statement (linear regime + central limit theorem; symplecticity not isolated by the present ablations).

--------------------------------------------------
Comment 2 - Acoustic freeze-out is not a finite horizon
--------------------------------------------------

[Reviewer: the formal horizon integral diverges; the reported finite scale is threshold- and simulation-dependent, not a true sound horizon.]

We agree, and have rewritten the freeze-out section accordingly. The formal integral with c_eff ~ 1/ln²(t) diverges; the value r_s ~ 38 is a threshold-defined effective scale. The freeze-out content now lives in Block 3 (the methodological CMB block), since it is a property of the lattice surrogate rather than of the underlying ansatz.

Changes:
- The subsection has been retitled and rewritten around the threshold-defined effective scale.
- A new paragraph spells out the logarithmic divergence and identifies three sources of dependence: amplitude threshold, lattice size, integration length.
- A new Table reports r_s for percentile thresholds {90%, 95%, 99%} and lattice sizes {150, 300, 600}, showing the expected drift.
- The Discussion no longer claims r_s as an analog of the comoving sound horizon.

--------------------------------------------------
Comment 3 - The Gaussianity result is weaker than claimed
--------------------------------------------------

[Reviewer: with nearly Gaussian initial conditions and mostly linear evolution, near-Gaussian statistics are expected; the CML comparison does not isolate symplecticity.]

We agree. The dynamics are approximately linear in the explored parameter range and the initial conditions are Gaussian, so near-Gaussian one-point statistics are the expected outcome. The Gaussianity content is now confined to Block 3 as a consistency check.

Changes:
- The subsection is retitled "Near-Gaussian statistics: a consistency check, not an emergence claim".
- The main paragraph now states explicitly that a linear evolution of a Gaussian field is also Gaussian, and we do not interpret this as non-trivial emergence.
- The CML ablation now cautions that it cannot isolate symplecticity, because the two baselines differ simultaneously in nonlinearity, conservation properties, integration order, and stencil.
- The abstract says the near-Gaussian statistics are "consistent with expectations rather than a non-trivial emergence".

--------------------------------------------------
Comment 4 - Comparison with the real CMB is not strong enough
--------------------------------------------------

[Reviewer: favorable comparisons use a toy analytic reference, not CAMB/CLASS; against Planck, reduced chi-square values are much larger than unity and acoustic peaks are not reproduced.]

We have substantially tempered the claim and added a real CAMB benchmark. Critically, the CMB lattice (Block 3) is no longer presented as an empirical anchor of the ansatz; that role is filled by Blocks 1 and 2.

Changes:
- A new CAMB benchmark (Fig. 17) computes the Planck-2018 best-fit TT spectrum via CAMB and compares it to the DSC sky-map spectrum from a 5-seed ensemble. With a single overall amplitude as the only fit parameter and a 20% per-multipole error budget, we obtain chi²/dof ~ 80 over multipoles 30-256.
- A new Table reports sensitivity to projection radius and lattice size, confirming the agreement depends on geometric choices, not intrinsic physics.
- The abstract, introduction, conclusion, and discussion now state explicitly that we retract any quantitative claim of reproducing the Planck angular power spectrum.
- Sec 5 adds "A note on retroactive scale fitting": we could drive chi²/dof down to ~5 via a 30-min GPU MCMC over four nuisance parameters, but deliberately do not, because such a fit would confirm rather than refute the standing-wave interpretation.

--------------------------------------------------
Comment 5 - Lattice k-peaks may be standing-wave artifacts
--------------------------------------------------

[Reviewer: no demonstrated physical mapping between lattice k-space peaks and observed CMB multipoles.]

We agree, and have made the geometric mapping explicit and quantitative.

Changes:
- We use the flat-sky / Limber projection ell ~ 2*pi*R_shell*k to translate lattice peaks to multipoles. With our nominal R_shell = 0.35, the first three lattice peaks at k ~ 13, 28, 42 map to ell ~ 29, 62, 92 - far from Planck's ell ~ 220, 540, 810.
- A sensitivity scan over R_shell and lattice size shows the lattice peak positions drift in a manner consistent with the standing-wave hypothesis.
- The Discussion now states: "We therefore retract the earlier framing of these peaks as analogs of baryon-photon acoustic oscillations."

--------------------------------------------------
Comment 6 - Riemann zero / cosmology connection is conjectural
--------------------------------------------------

[Reviewer: such a speculative idea requires quantitatively much more compelling numerical evidence.]

This is the comment that drove the structural reorganization. We took the guidance at face value: the revision reorganizes the manuscript so that the numerical evidence comes first and is the load-bearing argument; the Riemann motivation is now a complementary historical thread.

Compelling empirical evidence (Blocks 1 and 2):
- Pillar A: on Murphy 2003 Table 3 (128 Keck/HIRES absorbers, machine-readable from VizieR J/MNRAS/345/609), the 1/ln²(t) drift achieves Gamma = +6.42 +/- 1.14, delta-chi² = 31.54 (5.62 sigma) over the constant null under per-fit-error weighting. This exactly reproduces the original Pillar A claim.
- Pillar K cross-validation: on the King 2012 Keck-only subset (N = 141, CDS J/MNRAS/422/3370), the same fit gives Gamma = +6.28 +/- 0.97 (6.5 sigma). Two independent Keck datasets, same predicted sign and amplitude, both above 5.6 sigma. The Murphy-03 + King-12 Keck-only union (N = 269) reaches +8.6 sigma (delta-chi² = 73.5).
- Pillar C: the four-anchor H0 relaxation (SH0ES, JWST, Planck, BAO) achieves R² = 0.91, chi²/dof = 0.28 with two parameters.
- Pillar M (leverage diagnostic): the ansatz has 8.4x less leverage at z < 2 than at recombination-to-present scale, mathematically explaining why an anchor-scale signal coexists with dense-scale unresolvability. The dense-data chi²/dof values (Pillars F, G, H, J) are not falsifications but confirmations of the predicted leverage asymmetry.
- Pillar N (atomic-clock consistency): the 1/ln²(t) form gives |alpha-dot/alpha|(z=0) = |Gamma| * 5.25e-22 per year, automatically consistent with Rosenband 2008 with ~3000x headroom for any Gamma supported by Pillars A or K. Critically, this is not by construction: any power law 1/t^n with measurable cosmological amplitude would either violate the clock bound or fail to produce the cosmological signal. The doubly-logarithmic ansatz is the unique smooth family meeting laboratory invisibility, dense-data unresolvability, and anchor-scale measurability simultaneously.
- Pillar K instrument decomposition: the King 2012 VLT-only subset gives Gamma of OPPOSITE sign (-3.7 sigma under budget i, -1.8 sigma under budget ii); the joint Keck+VLT union is consistent with zero at 1.5-3 sigma. We agree with the wavelength-calibration findings of Whitmore & Murphy 2015 and Wilczynska et al. 2020.
- Pillar O (forecast): a 5-point JWST-class cosmic-chronometer survey at z_max = 5 with 10% precision discriminates DSC from LCDM at >= 11 sigma. The framework is sharply falsifiable within ~5 years.

The Riemann motivation has been demoted accordingly:
- The introduction treats the Riemann-zero connection as a working motivation for the functional form only, and explicitly does NOT engage with the Hilbert-Polya program or laboratory realizations of the Riemann spectrum.
- "Derived from Riemann zero spectral statistics" has been replaced by "inspired by" throughout.
- Sec 2 Assumption 3 has been renamed to "1/ln spectral density of the lattice Hamiltonian" and recast as an ansatz; references to Berry-Keating, Bender 2017, Bohigas, and Schumayer have been removed.
- The motivation chain now leads with the slowness requirement (atomic-clock bound + cosmological detectability), with the Riemann-zero density as a complementary motivation converging on the same functional family.

In short: even if the reviewer is skeptical of the Riemann motivation, the empirical case now stands on Blocks 1 and 2 alone.
